## The pool you can't rug

Latch is a Uniswap v4 Hook that locks launch liquidity onchain so the creator **physically cannot pull it**. A premature or oversized liquidity removal does not get taxed — it **reverts**. Liquidity unlocks gradually on a public, immutable vesting schedule, and a registry lets buyers verify a pool is Latch-locked *before* they buy.

<Note>
  Latch enforces its guarantee in pure pool state: no oracle, no identity check, no admin key. The gate fires regardless of who owns or transfers the LP position.
</Note>

## The wedge: they tax the dump, we make the rug revert

The crowded approach in this hackathon is a **dynamic or decaying swap fee** — economically deterring a dump by making it expensive. The problem: a determined seller just pays the fee and dumps anyway. Economics deter; they do not guarantee.

Latch is a different class. It changes **what the pool guarantees, not what it charges.**

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Fee hooks tax the dump" icon="coins">
    A decaying fee makes a rug expensive. A motivated rugger pays it and pulls liquidity regardless. The exit stays open.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Latch makes the rug revert" icon="lock">
    An over-schedule liquidity removal hits a `revert`, not a charge. The transaction fails. The exit is physically closed until the schedule says otherwise.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Info>
  Latch never touches the swap fee. `getHookPermissions()` enables **only** `beforeRemoveLiquidity`. It is deliberately not a fee hook.
</Info>

## What a liquidity lock does and does not do

We are scrupulously honest about scope. A liquidity lock closes exactly **one** rug vector — the hard liquidity pull. On its own it does not stop a supply dump, a malicious token, or a creator seeding a second unprotected pool. We close most of those for launcher pools and disclose the rest plainly.

<Card title="Read the honest scope" icon="scale-balanced" href="/concepts/honest-scope">
  We do not claim foolproof. See exactly which vectors Latch closes, which it mitigates, and which remain.
</Card>
